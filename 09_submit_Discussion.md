# Discussion

## Speed–Reliability Tradeoff and Operational Window Selection

The results confirm a fundamental tradeoff in single-station intensity forecasting: longer observation windows improve probability of detection (POD) and F1 but progressively shift the model's role from anticipatory warning to post-threshold confirmation. Anticipatory positive recall measures the fraction of stations that will ultimately reach intensity ≥4 but have not yet observed ≥4 at the prediction time—the persistence baseline cannot detect these by construction. At the 10-second window, SSIF correctly anticipates 8.8% of 3,102 pre-threshold stations; by the 40-second window, anticipatory recall rises to 43.8%, but the pool of pre-threshold stations shrinks to 630 because most stations that will reach ≥4 have already locally observed it. The model's anticipatory ability improves with more data, yet the opportunity to provide advance warning diminishes because the shaking has already arrived at most stations.

Lead time measures how many seconds before the station locally observes ≥4 the prediction is issued. At the 20-second window, the median lead time is 6 s, with 60.1% of detections providing ≥5 s and 26.1% providing ≥10 s—operationally significant for high-speed rail braking, elevator floor-locking, gas shutoff, and industrial safety shutdowns. At the 40-second window, however, the median lead time drops to 4 s and the fraction providing ≥5 s falls to 40.6%, because most ≥4 stations have already crossed the threshold locally and the model's correct detections increasingly represent confirmation rather than advance warning. This tradeoff is asymmetric: longer windows never reduce the total number of correct detections since POD is monotonic, but they systematically reduce the fraction of detections providing meaningful advance warning.

These results identify the 20-second window as the most appropriate operational balance for on-site EEW deployment. At this window, station-level POD reaches 0.512 while region-based event-level POD reaches 1.000, meaning all region-positive events are captured after spatial aggregation while approximately half of affected stations are individually detected. Earlier windows prioritize speed but miss more region-positive events; later windows detect nearly all ≥4 stations but shift toward confirmation. The 20-second window is where SSIF first achieves complete region-based event detection while retaining meaningful advance warning for a majority of detections, making it a practical supplementary pathway alongside eBEAR's rapid initial alerts when source characterization succeeds.
