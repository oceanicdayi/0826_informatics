# Intensity Observation and Alert Formulation

We compiled event-based instrumental intensity records from the Central Weather Administration (CWA) of Taiwan, comprising 964 unique earthquake events. Each event is archived as a 120 s, 1 Hz intensity time series for every reporting station, with several hundred stations contributing per event (47–614 in the testing cohort). The archive also includes station metadata (station code, name, county, coordinates, elevation) and earthquake catalog parameters (origin time, longitude, latitude, depth, magnitude). Critically, the catalog parameters and epicentral distances are retained for stratified evaluation but are never supplied as model inputs, ensuring that the forecaster operates purely on observed intensity without dependence on source-parameter estimation, ground-motion prediction equations, or station-to-source distances.

Instrumental intensity follows the 10-level CWA scale (0–9), where class 4 corresponds to strong shaking and serves as the operational alert threshold. For each station–event pair, the model receives an intensity sequence truncated to an origin-referenced early window of 10–40 s at 5 s increments (EW10–EW40). A binary validity mask of equal length accompanies the input, marking valid observations (1) and missing time steps (0); approximately 15% of time steps are missing in the testing cohort due to station outages. The prediction target is the maximum valid intensity over the full 120 s record—the highest CWA class reached at any valid time step. A station is classified as positive (alert) if its maximum valid intensity reaches or exceeds class 4 and negative otherwise.

The dataset exhibits severe class imbalance, reflecting the operational reality of earthquake early warning. In the 147-event testing cohort comprising 76,565 station–event records, only 3,816 records (5.0%) have a final intensity at or above class 4. The magnitude distribution is heavily skewed toward small events: 40% of all earthquakes have magnitude below 4 and rarely produce CWA intensity ≥4 at any station, while only four events (0.4%) reach magnitude 7 or above, including the 2024 M7.2 Hualien earthquake. This imbalance means that the most critical targets for early warning—large, damaging earthquakes—are the rarest in the training data, reinforcing the need for generalizable intensity-trajectory features rather than event-specific memorization.
