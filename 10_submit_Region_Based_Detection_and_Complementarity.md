# Region-Based Event Detection and eBEAR Complementarity

The any-station aggregation rule—an event is detected if any station anywhere predicts an alert—is not operationally meaningful for on-site EEW. We therefore define a region-based criterion anchored to 47 reference points covering all counties and major cities in Taiwan, each defining a circular alert region of 30 km radius. A region is classified as region-positive if at least 3 stations within the radius have actual final CWA intensity ≥4, and SSIF-detected if at least 3 stations are predicted to alert. This criterion requires spatial consistency among co-located stations rather than relying on a single isolated trigger. Among 50 matched testing events, 39 were region-positive and 11 were region-negative. From the 25-second window onward, SSIF achieves perfect performance: zero misses, zero false alarms, and F1 = 1.000. At the 20-second window, SSIF detects all 39 region-positive events (POD = 1.000) with only 3 false alarms (precision = 0.929). At 10 seconds, SSIF detects 34 of 39 (POD = 0.872) with zero false alarms, demonstrating that even with minimal observation the model produces reliable spatially consistent detections.

Under the same region-based criterion, eBEAR achieved a fixed POD of 0.949 (2 misses out of 39 region-positive events) with 7 false alarms (precision = 0.841, below the 0.90 operational target). eBEAR's alert decision is made once at the source-parameter estimation stage and does not improve with additional observation time. SSIF at the 20-second window matches eBEAR's POD while exceeding it in precision, and from 25 seconds onward surpasses eBEAR on all metrics. The complementarity is most clearly visible at the 20-second window: eBEAR misses 2 region-positive events, SSIF misses none, and the union POD reaches 1.000—no region-positive event is missed by both systems simultaneously. The 2 SSIF-only events are earthquakes where eBEAR's source-parameter estimation produced online magnitudes near the triggering threshold but did not issue a regional alert, while at least 3 stations within a 30 km region observed and were predicted to reach intensity ≥4, illustrating that eBEAR's errors stem from source-characterization uncertainty while SSIF's errors stem from insufficient observation time.

Anticipatory positive recall and retrospective lead time quantify the model's genuine predictive value beyond post-threshold confirmation. Anticipatory recall—detections of stations that will ultimately reach ≥4 but have not yet observed it at the prediction time—rises from 8.8% at 10 seconds to 43.8% at 40 seconds, while the pool of pre-threshold stations shrinks from 3,102 to 630. At the 20-second window, SSIF anticipates 14.6% of 2,173 pre-threshold stations, and the median retrospective lead time is 6 seconds, with 60.1% of detections providing ≥5 seconds—operationally significant for high-speed rail braking, elevator floor-locking, and industrial shutdowns. The training–testing F1 gap is largest at early windows (−0.077 at 10 seconds) and vanishes at longer windows, reversing to a slight positive gap at 35–40 seconds. A maximum gap of 7.8 percentage points is modest for a deep learning model, confirming that the event-disjoint split and two-stage model selection provide an unbiased generalization estimate, and that the model learns transferable intensity-prediction patterns rather than memorizing training-event-specific features.
