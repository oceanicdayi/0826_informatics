## Abstract

Taiwan's earthquake early warning (EEW) currently relies mainly on the source-parameter-based Earthworm-Based Earthquake Alarm Reporting system (eBEAR). Because eBEAR estimates earthquake location and magnitude from the earliest observations, errors in these initial source parameters can propagate into predicted shaking intensities, particularly for offshore earthquakes and complex ruptures. We developed the Second-by-second Seismic Intensity Forecaster (SSIF), a single-station multitask Transformer model that predicts final peak CWA intensity at each reporting station from that station's own 1 Hz intensity sequence observed within origin-referenced early windows of 10–40 s at 5 s intervals. Seven independent models were trained, one for each early window length (10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, and 40 s), on 670 event-disjoint Taiwanese earthquakes and evaluated on 147 held-out earthquakes comprising 76,565 station–event records per window. SSIF uses neither epicenter, magnitude, focal depth, source-to-station distance, waveform data, nor ground-motion prediction equations as input; the only per-station features are the scaled intensity class and a validity mask. At the CWA intensity class ≥4 alert threshold, SSIF achieved precision ≥ 0.93 and probability of detection (POD) rising from 0.257 at the 10-second window to 0.907 at the 40-second window; F1 improved monotonically from 0.40 to 0.93. A persistence baseline (predicting that the final class equals the maximum observed within the window) had perfect precision but consistently lower POD, confirming that SSIF extracts genuine predictive information beyond mere observation persistence. For event-level evaluation, we define a region-based criterion using 47 predefined reference points as centers for on-site EEW alert regions (30 km radius, ≥3 stations with I≥4). In a retrospective comparison with the operational eBEAR system, 50 of 147 testing events were matched to the eBEAR operational alert records; of these, 39 were region-positive. SSIF achieved region-based POD of 1.000 from the 20-second window onward with zero misses, while eBEAR maintained a fixed POD of 0.949 (2 misses) with 7 false alarms (precision 0.841). SSIF detected 2 region-positive events that eBEAR missed, and the union of both systems eliminated all misses (union POD = 1.000). Although SSIF may issue alerts later than eBEAR, it provides an independent observation-based pathway that complements the source-based system.
